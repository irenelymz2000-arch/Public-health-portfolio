# Mental Health Service Utilization Among Undergraduate Students

## Project overview

This mixed methods study examined undergraduate students' perceived need for mental health support, awareness of campus resources, and use of university mental health services. It was completed for PUBH 465 at the University of St. Thomas under the supervision of Professor Deanna Tollefson.

The study was conducted collaboratively by Najma Abade, Seham Hassen, Irene Lyimo, and Adna Yusuf. Irene independently developed and wrote the manuscript based on the completed collaborative research.

## Research question

How do awareness, accessibility, trust, cultural relevance, and structural barriers influence undergraduate students' use of campus mental health services?

## Methods

- Mixed methods, cross-sectional study
- Anonymous Qualtrics survey completed by 29 undergraduate students
- Semi-structured interviews with 6 undergraduate students
- Descriptive and cross-tabulation analysis using IBM SPSS Statistics
- Framework analysis of interview responses
- Joint display integrating quantitative and qualitative findings

Participation was voluntary, no identifying information was collected, and the course research project was exempt from full institutional review board review.

## Key findings

- 55.2% of survey participants reported needing mental health support during the previous 12 months.
- 27.6% reported using University of St. Thomas mental health services.
- 96.6% reported awareness of available campus mental health services.
- Reported barriers included limited knowledge about how to access services, time and scheduling constraints, stigma, uncertainty about cultural understanding, and a preference to handle concerns independently.

These descriptive findings suggest that knowing services exist does not necessarily lead students to use them. Trust, cultural responsiveness, scheduling, and clear information about how to begin seeking help may influence service utilization.

## Public health relevance

Universities can strengthen student support by communicating about services throughout the academic year, improving culturally responsive care, reducing scheduling barriers, and providing clear guidance about how students can begin accessing support.

## Limitations

The study used a small convenience sample from one university. Findings are descriptive and should not be generalized to all undergraduate students or used to establish causality.
