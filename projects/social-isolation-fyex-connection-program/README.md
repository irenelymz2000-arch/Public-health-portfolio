# UST FYEX Connection Program and First-Year Connectors

## Project overview

This collaborative public health capstone proposal addressed social isolation and loneliness among first-year students at the University of St. Thomas. The project team developed a peer-led program designed to strengthen belonging, social connection, campus engagement, and awareness of support resources.

The project was completed collaboratively by Aisha Dirie, Eden Krajewski, and Irene Lyimo for PUBH 485.

## Public health problem

Students entering college may experience loneliness and social isolation while adapting to new academic expectations, relationships, and environments. Limited social support can affect mental health, academic adjustment, engagement, and overall well-being.

## Proposed program

The UST FYEX Connection Program would embed trained upperclass students, called First-Year Connectors, in first-year experience seminars. Proposed activities included:

- Small peer groups led by First-Year Connectors
- Weekly community-building activities
- Individual student check-ins
- Movement-based and social activities outside class
- Digital communication and reflection through university platforms
- Referrals and connections to campus resources

This was a program proposal. The intervention had not yet been implemented or evaluated when the portfolio materials were prepared.

## Evidence and planning approach

The team used public health literature and available Minnesota and national student health information to examine loneliness, social connection, mental health, and student belonging. The proposal also included:

- A literature review
- Comparison of relevant Minnesota and national indicators
- A proposed student survey using the UCLA Loneliness Scale
- A logic model connecting resources, activities, outputs, outcomes, and long-term impact
- A research poster and slide presentation

If implemented and evaluated, the program could help increase peer interaction, improve students' awareness of campus resources, strengthen belonging, support help-seeking, and reduce social isolation among first-year students.
